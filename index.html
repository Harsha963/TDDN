TDDN: Text-aligned Diffused DINO Network for Puzzle Understanding

1Eightfold AI, 2Inception42, 3IIT Kharagpur
*Equal contribution
Paper arXiv Code Puzzle Perception

Patch features projected onto their top three principal components. CLIP produces fragmented, spatially inconsistent maps on structured images. DiffusedDINO keeps CleanDIFT's boundary precision while recovering DINOv3's object-level signal, and TDDN preserves that structure after text alignment.

Abstract

Structured visual reasoning, such as image puzzles, demands fine-grained visual perception, an ability current Vision Language Models (VLMs) largely lack. VLMs built on CLIP-based ViT backbones trade fine-grained detail for high-level semantics, and we show this loss propagates downstream.

To recover it, we fuse ViT (DINOv3) and Diffusion (CleanDIFT) representations into a perception encoder (DiffusedDINO) and align it with the RoBERTa-L language model, yielding a text-aligned model (TDDN) that preserves this perceptual advantage: with frozen backbones and only ~590K alignment pairs, TDDN matches CLIP on image-text retrieval while tripling its dense-prediction accuracy (ADE20K 5.20 → 18.11 mIoU, COCO-Stuff 7.35 → 24.44), despite CLIP's training on a massive corpus. TDDN leads on segmentation benchmarks among general-purpose contrastive encoders, including SigLIP 2. We further introduce Puzzle Perception, a puzzle-based segmentation and visual question answering dataset that probes fine-grained spatial understanding, on which TDDN doubles CLIP's segmentation accuracy (11.04 → 22.51 mIoU).

Method

DiffusedDINO pairs CleanDIFT decoder activations from layers {2, 5, 8}, which resolve fine spatial boundaries without noise or timestep tuning, with DINOv3 final-layer patch tokens, which stay semantically organized and patch-consistent. Each CleanDIFT layer is projected to a common 512-d space, concatenated, and fused with the DINOv3 patch tokens; the fused tokens plus the DINOv3 CLS token pass through two trainable self-attention blocks.

TDDN architecture. Frozen CleanDIFT UNet and DINOv3 ViT-H/16+ feed a trainable fusion path; RoBERTa-L is frozen on the text side. Only the per-layer MLPs, the fusion MLP, the self-attention blocks, and the text projection are trained (~80M parameters, 5,000 iterations, 4×A100, ~15 hours).

Text alignment follows the low-data recipe of STRUCTURE: a symmetric InfoNCE objective plus a regulariser that preserves the pairwise geometry of each pretrained latent space. Because every backbone stays frozen and the objective, text encoder, data, and schedule are held fixed across conditions, any gain over CLIP is attributable to the fused representation rather than a stronger objective or text backbone.

Key insights

The two sources split the work by granularity

At the patch level the fused representation is closest to CleanDIFT (CKA 0.96, PWCCA 1.00); at the global level it is closest to DINOv3 (CKA 0.81, PWCCA 0.99). Variance partitioning confirms neither source is recoverable from the other, so CleanDIFT supplies patch structure while DINOv3 anchors global semantics.

Fusion buys balance, not one axis

Single encoders trade alignment against uniformity: DINOv3 has the best alignment (0.33) but the worst uniformity (−1.10), while CleanDIFT and SD show the reverse. DiffusedDINO takes the best joint position (0.79, −2.12), which is what the downstream gains track.

Alignment does not have to erase perception

Freezing the backbones and adding the STRUCTURE regulariser keeps the pretrained geometry intact through contrastive training. The dense advantage survives text alignment with 590K pairs against CLIP's 400M, so the gap comes from the representation rather than alignment scale or a task-specific dense head.

The classification gap is coverage, not quality

Zero-shot classification trails CLIP because 590K captions cannot cover hundreds of visually diverse categories, and the gap is largest on GTSRB, a concept largely absent from our captions. TIP-Adapter with 16 shots per class closes most of it, lifting Caltech-101 from 79.66 to 90.50, past CLIP's 86.49.

Puzzle Perception

Existing segmentation benchmarks cover natural images with coarse semantics. Puzzle Perception pairs pixel-level annotations with puzzle-based VQA across four structured domains, rendered at 512×512 over 30 classes, with masks taken directly from the rendering pipeline so there is no annotation noise. No Puzzle Perception image appears in the alignment corpus, so all reported results on it are zero-shot transfer.
